Construction Tax

From percentage-of-completion results to on-site labour documentation and the financial requirements for keeping your licence. Construction is taxed in a fundamentally different way from other industries.

OVERVIEW

Overview

In construction, projects span fiscal years and every site has a different cost structure, which makes matching the timing of profit and loss difficult. On top of that, maintaining construction registration requires meeting capital and financial-ratio thresholds at every closing, and a single flawed record for on-site daily labour can trigger assessments of both withholding tax and VAT. Establishing site-level controls from the outset is the surest form of tax saving.

VALUE Tax & Accounting first designs a site-level cost structure, distinguishing between general and specialty construction and between public and private contracts. We align progress billing and tax invoice timing with the terms of each contract, and we simulate real net worth and financial ratios before closing so that licence maintenance is never at risk. For daily-worker labour costs, we also put reporting in order so that attendance records and cash flows line up with each other.

KEY ISSUES

Key tax issues in construction

  1. 01

    Percentage-of-completion revenue

    At every filing you must reconcile the difference between accounting profit based on percentage of completion and the timing of recognition for tax purposes. If the client's progress-confirmation schedule and your book entries diverge, an entire tax period can shift, so the recognition date should be fixed against the progress terms written into the contract.

  2. 02

    On-site labour documentation

    For daily-worker labour costs, a single mismatch among qualifying evidence, withholding tax and the four major insurances leads straight to an assessment. If you keep payment statements but leave no actual cash trail, the expense is treated as fabricated payroll and disallowed, so attendance logs and bank transfer records must be managed together.

  3. 03

    Maintaining construction registration

    A licence is maintained only if the capital, technician and real net worth requirements are met at every closing. Real net worth is judged on the finalised financial statements, so it is hard to undo once the books are closed, and any provisional payments or irrecoverable receivables accumulated during the year become direct deductions.

  4. 04

    Subcontract and material costs

    The timing of tax invoice receipt, the input VAT credit and the flow of subcontract payments must be managed together. Amounts paid without qualifying evidence lose the credit and attract a penalty for missing documentation, so a check on whether evidence has been received belongs in the payment process itself.

  5. 05

    Cost allocation by site

    Long-term projects that span fiscal years require a precise split between work-in-progress and completed-work accounts and between the costs of each site. Allocating common costs arbitrarily distorts site-level results, causes progress billings and taxable income to diverge, and leaves you without a defensible basis when substantiation is later requested.

FAQ

Construction tax FAQ

Q When is the real net worth needed to keep a construction licence assessed? expand_more

It is judged on the financial statements at each closing. Because it is hard to undo once the books are closed, it is safer to keep provisional payments and bad debts from accumulating during the year and to run a simulation before closing.

Q What documents are required for on-site daily labour costs? expand_more

Payment statements for daily wage income must be filed monthly, and the attendance log must agree with the actual bank transfer records. If the statements exist but no cash moved, the expense is treated as fabricated payroll, disallowed, and withholding tax may be assessed on top.

Q We received a progress payment — when should the tax invoice be issued? expand_more

For long-term contract work, the time of supply is the date progress is confirmed under the contract, not the date payment is actually received. If the client's inspection schedule and your issuance date diverge, a late-issuance penalty applies, so check the progress-confirmation terms in the contract first.

Q We paid a subcontractor but never received a tax invoice. expand_more

Amounts paid without qualifying evidence cannot be credited as input VAT and attract a penalty for missing documentation. If the counterparty refuses to issue an invoice, you can use the purchaser-issued tax invoice procedure, so act quickly from the time of payment.

Q Can construction firms claim the special SME tax reduction? expand_more

Construction is among the industries eligible for the reduction. The rate varies with the location of the business and the size of the company, however, and there are cases where it cannot be combined with other credits, so calculate which combination is more favourable before choosing.
